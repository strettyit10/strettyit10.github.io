Educational objectiveQID 37
STEMI involves transmural (full-thickness) infarction of the myocardial wall, and usually results from acute atherosclerotic plaque rupture with the development of overlying thrombus that fully occludes the coronary artery lumen. Educational objectiveQID 193
LV free wall rupture is an uncommon but devastating mechanical complication of transmural MI that occurs within 5 days or up to 2 weeks following the event. Educational objectiveQID 557
Chronic granulomatous disease is an X-linked disorder resulting from deficiency of NADPH oxidase, the enzyme responsible for formation of reactive oxygen species in phagosomes. Neutrophils affected by this disorder are unable to kill catalase-producing organisms, resulting in recurrent bacterial and fungal infections that frequently involve the lungs, skin, and lymph nodes.

Educational objectiveQID 14921
Acute phase reactants are proteins whose serum concentrations change by ≥25% during periods of inflammation. Procalcitonin is a unique acute phase reactant that can rise or fall depending on the etiology of the infection; elevated levels correlate with a bacterial source, whereas low levels indicate a viral source.
Q9

Post-exposure prophylaxis for meningococcal contacts

ID · public health

The goal of prophylaxis is eradicating nasopharyngeal carriage, not treating disease — that single distinction sorts every choice.
Option Eradicates carriage? Problem Choice
Rifampin Yes None — first-line. Warn about orange body fluids, contact lens staining, OCP failure via CYP450 induction D
Immediate vaccination No Takes 7–10 days for protection; adjunct in outbreaks, not post-exposure management A
Observation only — Household contacts carry hundreds of times the baseline risk; disease can be fulminant within hours B
Penicillin No Treats invasive disease but leaves carriage intact — the index patient also needs rifampin before discharge if treated with penicillin C
Sulfamethoxazole Historically Widespread resistance; abandoned E
